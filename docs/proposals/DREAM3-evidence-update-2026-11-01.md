# DREAM3: Evidence Update

*Development evidence supplement, 1 November 2026*

> Snapshot of saved development evidence on 1 November 2026. Later studies (continuity, direction screens, Stage A v2) are in [results/](../../results/).

## What the new data establishes

Two matched survival pilots have completed generation: Qwen3-8B and Mistral-7B each produced 72 three-call chains, giving 144 chains and 432 calls in total. Both pilots cover six development tasks, three seeds, and four arms: strong prompting, higher temperature, targeted steering, and random perturbation. Each arm contains 18 chains per model. All recorded calls ended with EOS; the recorded ideation schedules completed in all targeted and random-control chains. This establishes execution coverage, not useful originality.

## Ratings remain incomplete

At the saved-data snapshot, Qwen3 had 1,339 of 4,386 expected successful content-rating calls (30.53%); Mistral had 1,244 of 4,248 (29.28%). Neither pilot had saved lineage judgments. The primary endpoint needs content ratings and evidence that final ideas retain a substantive seed relationship, so an arm comparison is not yet available. Missing ratings must remain missing, with bounds where applicable; they cannot be counted as evidence of poor ideas. No pilot treatment ranking was computed for this update.

## Developer qualification: a floor

The separate developer-qualification set is more complete than the older status note indicates: all 1,116 content calls and 48 lineage calls are saved. Applying the existing frozen aggregation to 12 historical candidate sets, each forked into review-first and development-first branches, gives zero qualifying surviving ideas in both branches. The review branch delivered 4.83 final ideas per chain; development delivered 5.00. These are delivery counts, not qualifying-yield scores.

The declared rule switches to review only with at least 0.5 additional qualifying ideas and no lower delivery. Its observed difference is zero, so it retains development by default. Twelve source sets span only four underlying objects; this is a development decision, not evidence of workflow equivalence.

## What the floor means

Both judge families assigned novelty below 3 to all 118 delivered final ideas in the developer set. The primary rule requires novelty, value, and coherence of at least 3, plus constraints and lineage. Weak seeds, ineffective development, a stringent instrument, or a combination could explain this floor. Six item-by-judge groups include an excerpt that did not match verbatim; the flags are retained.

Diagnostic consistency supports repeatability on those items, not external validity. Changing thresholds after seeing these outcomes would require a dated amendment and fresh confirmation data.

## Older pairwise evidence

A completed review of the earlier decategorization pilot favors controls on material worth developing. Across both judges and answer orders, ordinary generation is preferred in 39 judgments, steering in 5, with 4 ties. Temperature wins 36 judgments, steering 9, with 3 ties. Each comparison comprises 48 repeated judgments over 12 objects.

Answer-order consistency was 16/24 for Claude and 20/24 for Codex. Three supporting excerpts across the questions were flagged as nonverbatim. This different historical workflow cannot substitute for the new matched survival endpoint.

## Implications for DREAM3

E1 now has completed pilot generation and a measurement warning, but no complete survival comparison. The intended E2–E5 experiments remain outstanding. A 7B model and an 8B model from different families cannot establish a size trend.

Keep the five papers prospective and complete pilot ratings and lineage before interpreting steering’s benefit. The original runs and proposals remain intact. This update uses saved records only and launches no model or judging calls.
