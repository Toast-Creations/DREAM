# Beyond Unusual Words

*DREAM3 · E4 · Validating useful originality*

> **Status: proposal, not a result.** This study has not been run. It is published as written on 1 November 2026 so the design can be read and criticised before any data exist. See [the proposals index](README.md).

David Cook · 1 November 2026 · Research proposal v1.1.0

## Abstract

We propose a prospective validation study connecting idea-quality judgments to independent task-specific evidence. The immediate priority is measurement: DREAM's scoped-v4 instrument completed all assessments but failed qualification in both judge families, and the legacy instrument confused set requirements with member requirements. We will test assessment scope, extraction equivalence, content dimensions, and lineage before evaluating whether a frozen rubric predicts independent task success better than semantic distance alone. No model judge or authored anchor is assumed to be ground truth.

## Rationale and qualification

Creativity measures can emphasize semantic separation without practical value \[1\]. Prior steering research and human-based creativity evaluation motivate separating novelty from usefulness \[2,3\]. Current DREAM data demonstrate that repeatability alone can preserve a scope error. Stage A therefore uses unseen matched representations, ordinary-but-useful and unusual-but-useless examples, task-family-specific positive/null cases, duplicates, mergers, and unrelated replacements. Include whole-set versus single-member presentations and equivalent numbering layouts. Authors' expected labels are checked independently rather than treated as external validation.

Freeze all candidate-instrument rules before qualification and use genuinely new fixtures after any amendment. Exact-score and endpoint-decision stability are different properties; select their tolerances prospectively. A failed version remains failed. A human-checked Stage A supports a technical qualification claim only; it does not establish predictive validity on real generated ideas. Do not choose a rubric because it gives steering a larger advantage.

## Prospective validation design

The planning Stage B sample contains 180 final slots: 20 new tasks × three generation arms × three seeds, with one of five slots selected uniformly before its text is inspected. Ten tasks concern constrained designs and ten structural analogies. All arms receive matched unsteered development. Calibration tasks, validation tasks, and later E1 confirmation tasks are disjoint. Missing selected outputs are not replaced by attractive examples. Keep delivery failure separate from missing assessor data.

## Independent checks and analysis

Three condition-masked human content raters assess novelty against a frozen reference pool, task value, and coherence. A separate assessor applies task checks specified before generation: executable low-stakes specifications where possible, or a documented relational mapping and derived prediction for analogies. Assessors do not see rubric scores or methods. Record executable and expert-only evidence separately. Artistic ideas require a separate family-appropriate human validation design; literal physical feasibility is not an artistic-quality criterion.

The primary estimand is the task-grouped held-out Brier-score difference between a semantic-distance predictor and the same model augmented with the frozen rubric decision. Lower scores indicate better probability prediction of independent check success. Freeze the embedding model, predictor class, calibration, grouped folds, missing-output treatment, and uncertainty method. Fit and calibrate only within training folds; all members of a task remain together. With only twenty independent tasks, report wide uncertainty and use development simulation to finalize task count.

Report sensitivity, specificity, score distributions, judge/human agreement, and task-family results. Independently evaluate lineage on paired seed/final examples; content validation does not validate the survival link. Qualification and validation data cannot subsequently become fresh E1 confirmation evidence. Deterministic test success measures constrained performance, not originality; a conventional workable proposal may pass value while failing novelty.

## Decision and scope

Run Stage B only if the candidate instrument passes its fixed technical gates. Validate its association with independent usefulness without requiring a favorable steering effect. If validation fails, revise prospectively and use a new validation sample, or retain a transparently labeled AI-rating study without claiming human utility. Budget Stage A first to avoid repeating thousands of uninformative calls. Release fixtures, task checks, selected-slot IDs, raw judgments, disagreements, and all revisions. This is the first dependency for E1–E3, not a cosmetic follow-up to an effectiveness claim.
