## Registration and public credit

A submitted preregistration records a study plan; an editable draft is not the same thing. OSF supports embargo settings for limited disclosure. A private or embargoed record is not a publicly discoverable priority announcement.

If public research credit is the goal, publish an accurately named versioned research record and software release, complete CITATION.cff with real identifiers, and link the protocol, source, and results. Zenodo registers a DOI when a record is published. A reserved DOI alone is not publication.

No DOI, ORCID, lab affiliation, registration, repository URL, patent number, or endorsement is invented in this package. No public release or contact with another lab has been performed as part of the rewrite.

## Licensing

The package includes the complete standard Apache License 2.0 and a separate NOTICE. This follows the original packages' stated software license. Third-party models and libraries have their own terms; this package does not relicense their weights or code.

Apache-2.0 permits broad reuse under its terms. Its notice requirements do not guarantee a citation in every paper that uses a general idea. A citation request and transparent contribution evidence help scholarly attribution but are not exclusive ownership of the field.

Copyright protects eligible expression rather than an underlying method or scientific idea. Human authorship and AI-generated content also need to be distinguished. This document does not decide copyright eligibility or ownership.

## Confidentiality and patents

If possible patent protection matters, get advice before public disclosure or changing the publication strategy. The USPTO notes that prefiling disclosure can jeopardize foreign rights and describes a limited U.S. grace period for certain inventor disclosures. A provisional is not an examined, granted patent.

This package is not a freedom-to-operate opinion, inventorship determination, or patentability search. Ordinary research overlap should be handled through citations and narrow claims; use of restricted material, contractual terms, and patent claims are separate questions.
