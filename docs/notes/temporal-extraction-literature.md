# Symbolic engines help, but parsing still breaks

No: as of November 2026, nobody has solved the problem DREAM's temporal branch keeps hitting, and no published work even measures it directly. No benchmark asks an LLM to label a report as a physical event, a state observation, or a revision of an earlier report, and then extract typed records. Every neighbouring benchmark (state-change tracking, temporal relations, event factuality, belief revision) shows prompted LLMs well short of reliable. Several of DREAM's specific error classes match biases the 2024-2026 literature now documents: models read goal-directed events as completed results, they over-update when they should hold a belief steady, and their entity binding degrades as distractors pile up. The end goal fares better. New work from 2025-2026 supports the division of labour DREAM uses, an LLM parser feeding an explicit symbolic state, and supports choosing what to check by value of information rather than by uncertainty. The strongest caution is that symbolic correctness ends where the parser begins. Text-to-PDDL output can be 94% solvable and only 25% semantically correct, and no paper measures unsafe actions caused by confidently wrong text extraction feeding a symbolic planner. DREAM's 5-in-1,536 unsafe goal copies are therefore an uncommon kind of evidence rather than a known quantity. The literature points to three next steps: a 32B-class or fine-tuned extractor, an explicit gate on revisions, and mandatory verification keyed to how critical a decision is rather than to model confidence.

## No benchmark tests DREAM's three-way label, and every neighbour is unsolved

The closest benchmarks each cover one slice of DREAM's label space, and prompted LLMs fall short on all of them. On procedural state change, OpenPI2.0 one-shot exact match on pre/post states is **0.074 for gpt-3.5-turbo and 0.225 for text-davinci-003**, and per-step schema F1 is only 0.025-0.155 ([arXiv 2305.14603](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.14603v2)). On temporal relations, zero-shot ChatGPT scores **25.3 F1 on MATRES**, rising to 52.4 with chain of thought, against roughly 76-88 for supervised systems ([arXiv 2304.05454](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.05454)). On event factuality, the 112k-event MAVEN-Fact dataset tops out at **47.6% macro-F1**, and LLMs collapse on the non-factual classes ([arXiv 2407.15352](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2407.15352)). On belief revision, most open models score about 0% on the update half of Belief-R, and GPT-4-Turbo, Claude 3 and Llama-3-70B reach at most about 50% on the combined score ([Belief-R, EMNLP 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.586.pdf)). The ICLR 2026 AGM-Bench finds that even o3-mini and DeepSeek-R1 systematically violate minimal-change and preservation postulates. Under iterated revision they show "belief inertia" (keeping retracted information) alongside "collateral damage" (dropping unaffected beliefs) ([ICLR 2026](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/10017503)).

The missing piece matters most for DREAM. Classical belief-change theory separates *revision* (correcting a wrong belief about an unchanged world) from *update* (the world itself changed), the Katsuno-Mendelzon distinction. The research found no LLM study that tests it. Belief-R, AGM-Bench and DeltaLogic all test revision alone. So DREAM's "removal read as a correction" error sits exactly in an unmeasured gap: nobody has shown how often LLMs confuse "the item was taken away" with "the earlier report was wrong". Benchmark validity is also contested. OpenPI-C criticises OpenPI's metric for rewarding repetition ([OpenPI-C](https://ml.paperswithcode.com/paper/openpi-c-a-better-benchmark-and-stronger)), ProPara-CRTS argues that the original ProPara annotations are too inconsistent for reliable evaluation ([IWCS 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.iwcs-main.22.pdf)), and a probe reported at 75% overall accuracy on Alchemy scored **3.1% on non-trivial state changes** because 87.6% of cases were "nothing changed" ([Kim & Schuster, ACL 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.02363)). That last point supports DREAM's practice of scoring arrivals, removals and corrections separately rather than reporting one pooled accuracy.

## DREAM's four error classes each match a documented bias

| DREAM error class | Closest published evidence | What it implies |
|---|---|---|
| Arrivals read as states (exclusive-kind 14-26% correct) | Teleological bias: 7-9B models call ambiguous accomplishments "completed" 94-100% of the time ([ACL 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.689.pdf)) | Outcome-focused wording ("now on hand") pulls toward the resulting state |
| Removals read as revisions | No study of update-vs-revision confusion; AGM-Bench shows revision itself is unreliable ([ICLR 2026](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/10017503)) | Unmeasured in the literature; DREAM's data is novel |
| Invented revisions when a report references an earlier record (18/24) | Belief-R update/maintain trade-off ([EMNLP 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.586.pdf)); offering many slots gives "high recall but low precision" ([EMNLP 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1743.pdf)) | Advertised slots get filled; put an explicit gate before the list |
| Rare wrong-entity bindings | Positional binding gets noisy for middle entities across Llama, Gemma and Qwen ([arXiv 2510.06182](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.06182)); GPT-4 contradiction-detection F1 23-53 ([AttrScore](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2305.06311)) | Exact quotes don't certify bindings; check bindings separately |

The arrival problem has the clearest analogue. Ma and Miyao's "Imperfective Paradox" study tested seven 7-9B models on progressive accomplishments whose completion is genuinely unknown. Six answered "completed" **94-100% of the time**, and accuracy on sentences with an explicit interruption was only **0.03-0.47**, meaning models ignore cancellation clauses ([ACL 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.689.pdf)). DREAM's failure points the other way at the surface: it labels the event as a state rather than inventing a completion. The mechanism plausibly matches, though. Arrival reports name the resulting condition, and the model latches onto the outcome rather than the transition. That is an inference; no paper tests resultative "arrived, now on hand" phrasing directly. OpenPI2.0 documents the mirror-image error, with models predicting generic changes such as location or cleanness that never occurred ([arXiv 2305.14603](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.14603v2)).

DREAM's intervention results also look like the literature's. Withholding history raised arrivals (28 to 49 of 120) but hurt removals and corrections (DREAM results). Ma and Miyao report the same whack-a-mole: definition-aware prompting lifted Qwen2.5-7B on telic cases while its atelic accuracy fell from about 0.97 to 0.72, and counterfactual prompting fixed telic cases while collapsing atelic accuracy to **0.00** for Llama-3.1 and Gemma-2 ([alphaXiv 2601.09373](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2601.09373.md)). The factored events/states/revisions schema plus deterministic compiler raised four-class exact meaning from 0.70 to 0.85 and cut accepted removal-as-revision errors from 12 to 0, but it left 18 of 24 context-dependent controls with invented revisions (DREAM results). That is the same profile as Belief-R, where models that update readily also update when they should not ([Belief-R](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.586.pdf)). The size of the factored-schema gain fits published schema-design effects. GoLLIE's code-style schemas with guideline docstrings raised average zero-shot IE F1 from **42.3 to 55.3** at 7B, and CASIE event detection from 33.9 to 59.3 ([arXiv 2310.03668](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.03668v5)). The nullable-prior schema had no measurable effect (DREAM results), which also fits: the research found no study in which nullability alone changed semantic accuracy, and GoLLIE's largest ablation effect came from "representative candidates" (worked examples), not type signatures.

## Scale and fine-tuning are the levers the evidence supports

Qwen3-8B is probably below a capability threshold. On the Imperfective Paradox, the Qwen2.5 family's teleological-bias rate moves from 0.53 at 7B and 0.61 at 14B to **0.09 at 32B** and 0.16 at 72B, a sharp jump the authors place between 14B and 32B ([alphaXiv 2601.09373](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2601.09373.md)). The evidence is one model family on one task, and extrapolating from Qwen2.5 to Qwen3 is untested. It is still the most direct size evidence available. Frontier scale does not close revision, though: Belief-R's best large models stay near 50% ([Belief-R](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.586.pdf)). The research found no Qwen3, Llama-4, GPT-5 or Claude 4.x results on any of the relevant benchmarks.

Fine-tuning has the largest published effect sizes for type-level decisions, and it works through existing circuits. Fine-tuning sharpens an LLM's existing entity-tracking circuit rather than building a new one ([ICLR 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14811)). A parameter-efficiently fine-tuned Llama-3-8B on a clean referent-tracking schema beats zero-shot LLMs on ProPara and approaches supervised systems ([IWCS 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.iwcs-main.22.pdf)). Prompted LLMs are consistently worse than fine-tuned small models in most few-shot IE settings ([Findings of EMNLP 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.08559)). The catch is that gains concentrate on type detection, not argument binding: GoLLIE added about 25 F1 on CASIE event detection but only about 2 on argument extraction ([arXiv 2310.03668](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.03668v5)). Fine-tuning should therefore help with arrivals and false revisions more than with wrong-entity bindings.

Decoding-level fixes will not touch DREAM's semantic errors. Grammar-constrained decoding is neutral to slightly positive for Llama-3.1-8B: on GSM8K, for example, it scored 80.1 unconstrained against 81.6-83.8 under the various engines ([JSONSchemaBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.10868v2)). The large losses attributed to JSON output mostly trace to answer fields placed before reasoning fields and to mismatched prompts ([Tam et al., EMNLP 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.02442v3); [dottxt rebuttal](https://blog.dottxt.ai/say-what-you-mean.html)). Constrained decoding buys validity, not meaning. Verification by quotation or entailment is weak exactly where DREAM needs it: GPT-4 few-shot reaches only 31-53 F1 on detecting contradicted attributions, and fine-grained mismatches account for 30.6% of its errors ([AttrScore](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2305.06311)). The best small grounding checker is wrong on roughly one judgment in four ([MiniCheck, EMNLP 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.10774v2)). These results suggest a split. Item-level voting across samples should prune DREAM's low-agreement invented revisions, while consistent wrong-entity bindings need a deterministic check of entity strings against record keys or a contrastive verifier. That split is an inference; no study measures self-consistency on event typing with 7-8B models.

## Hybrid parser-plus-engine designs win, but correctness by construction ends at the parser

The 2025-2026 literature has largely converged on DREAM's architecture. PDDL-Mind has an LLM parse narratives into PDDL and execute them against fixed update rules. Its authors report GPT-4o reaching **88.3% on MMToM-QA** and conclude that theory-of-mind benchmarks mainly penalise LLMs' "unreliable implicit implementation of parsing and state tracking" ([arXiv 2604.17819](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.17819), preprint). On memory, MERIT finds that update-on-write structured fact stores score **0.70-1.00 on updated facts against 0.30-0.95 for embedding retrieval** ([arXiv 2609.05441](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05441), preprint). Supersede shows that bounded LLM-managed memory loses 15-28 points against full context on knowledge updates even for gpt-5.4 ([arXiv 2606.27472](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.27472v1), preprint). On MemoryAgentBench, multi-hop conflict resolution stays in single digits for about 22 memory systems ([ICLR 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.05257v1)). Symbolic oversight of an LLM planner reached 99% on ALFWorld and 100% on a real quadruped against 50% for the pure LLM planner ([arXiv 2510.06357](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.06357v1)). The LLM+P regime, with a fixed domain file and the LLM writing only problem facts, reached 85-95% on simpler domains ([arXiv 2304.11477](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.11477)). DREAM's declared transitions and per-report extraction correspond to this easier regime.

The counter-evidence is sharp and points at DREAM's remaining weakness. In Planetarium, GPT-4o's text-to-PDDL output was **96.1% parseable and 94.4% solvable but only 24.8% semantically correct** ([NAACL 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.03321)). A symbolic engine faithfully propagates whatever the parser gives it, and validity checks cannot see wrong meaning. GPT-4 as a text world simulator gets non-trivial transitions right at most **59.9%** of the time, and it does worst on environment-driven changes ([ACL 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06485)). That argues for keeping background dynamics in DREAM's engine rather than asking the LLM to infer them. Retraction handling in hybrid systems remains primitive. EvoKG resolves contradictions by letting the higher-confidence fact win ([arXiv 2509.15464](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.15464v1)), and the research found no correction benchmark for LLM-to-KG or LLM-to-PDDL pipelines. DREAM's explicit, time-indexed revision records and supported-state sets are stronger than this baseline. However, they cannot protect against a confidently wrong single parse that leaves only the wrong state supported.

## Selective observation has value-of-information backing, but mostly for asking humans

Choosing what to check by decision relevance now has peer-reviewed support. Dong et al. compute expected utility gain against the cost of asking and match or beat tuned baselines across four domains ([ACL 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.06407v1)). REVOIR achieves greater success with fewer questions than prompting, chain-of-thought, fine-tuning and information-gain baselines ([NeurIPS 2026](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/173278)). KnowNo's conformal "act if the prediction set is a singleton, otherwise ask" cut human help by 10-24% at guaranteed success levels ([CoRL 2023](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v229/ren23a.html)). DREAM's dependency-aware selection, which queries only unresolved prerequisites of the current goal, is a cheap structural approximation of value of information. Its roughly +1.95 utility per world over 768 text-interface worlds (DREAM results) is consistent with this literature. One caveat bounds the comparison: nearly all of this work asks a *human* about *intent*, not the *world* about *state*, and its gains are mostly fewer questions at equal success rather than fewer unsafe actions.

The evidence against trusting the LLM to decide when to check is strong. On AgentAbstain, the best of 17 models reached only **59.5% paired act/abstain accuracy**, and 19.7% of should-abstain runs produced a confidently wrong deliverable ([arXiv 2607.10059](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.10059v1), preprint). False successes make up 45-48% of failures in single-control tau2-bench domains, and no LLM-judge configuration exceeded **AUROC 0.65** at detecting them ([arXiv 2606.09863](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09863), workshop). Even when agents retrieve the correct updated value, they act on it only **55% of the time** ([MERIT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05441)). On the mitigation side, SIMMER's symbolic kitchen world model found up to 56% of frontier plans contained latent failures, and explicit counterfactual state simulation cut those by up to 72% ([COLM 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14574)). VIRF's symbolic sandbox reached a **0% hazardous-action rate**, but on given state, not text-derived state ([ICLR 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08373)). The closest analogue to staleness-aware selective verification is CASEL. It routes each tool call using failure probability, consequence, reversibility and evidence freshness, and it halved harmful trajectories from 14.75% to 7.02%, but only in simulation, and its authors state that "intervention effectiveness is assumed, not measured" ([arXiv 2610.36570](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36570)).

That leaves DREAM in a sparsely occupied niche. The research found no paper that combines text-to-symbolic temporal extraction, budgeted environment queries chosen by dependency or value of information, and measured unsafe-action rates in one evaluation. DREAM's 5 unsafe copies out of 1,536, all traced to confident wrong-field extractions (DREAM results), are exactly the failure every source above predicts. Any query policy keyed on extraction confidence will miss these errors, because the model is not uncertain about them.

## Conclusion

The research changes how DREAM's errors should be read. They are not artefacts of one prompt or one schema. Each is the local form of a bias measured elsewhere, and the field has not even started measuring the update-versus-revision confusion. That makes DREAM's contrastive controls a potential contribution rather than just a debugging aid. The factored schema's gains, together with its remaining false revisions, have the same profile as published over-correction trade-offs. So more prompt engineering on an 8B model will probably keep moving errors between classes rather than removing them. The evidence-backed escalation is a 32B-class model or a small fine-tune with contrastive negative examples, an explicit "does this report retract an earlier one? quote it" gate placed before the revision list, and deterministic binding checks.

For the end goal, the new evidence favours the architecture and the query principle, but it shifts where safety has to come from. The symbolic engine and value-of-information selection both assume trustworthy inputs. Planetarium, AgentAbstain and the false-success work show that the inputs most likely to cause harm are the ones the model is confident about. The design implication is to make verification mandatory at points that are irreversible or that complete a goal, and to treat single-source, uncorroborated extractions as unresolved however confident the parser is. Dependency-aware querying then becomes a budget allocator layered over a fixed safety floor rather than the safety mechanism itself. Measuring that combination end to end, with harm traced to specific extraction errors, would fill a gap the 2023-2026 literature leaves open.

## Corrections after primary-source review (2026-11-06; appended, original text above unchanged)

A primary-source audit by David's reviewer (private review notes)
narrows several conclusions above. Read the report with these corrections.

1. **"Nobody measures this" / "no published work even measures it directly"** is unestablished. The supportable
   statement: an exact matching evaluation (event vs state vs retraction with DREAM's temporal contract and
   attributed decision failures) was **not found among the checked sources**. Related work already measures formal-
   translation semantics, changing-memory dependencies, perception errors and planning outcomes. A narrower
   contribution may exist; priority is not established. The update-vs-revision distinction itself is classical
   (Katsuno & Mendelzon).
2. **The 32B threshold is a hypothesis for DREAM, not a demonstrated threshold.** The cited result is Qwen2.5 on
   aspectual completion entailment, not Qwen3 on DREAM's extraction task. DREAM's error (event omission with a
   correct result state) differs from completion bias; a common mechanism is an analogy to test.
3. **The fine-tuning evidence is less decisive.** GoLLIE's 42.3→55.3 compares two *trained* CodeLLaMA systems (with
   and without guideline-aware representation), not an untouched model vs fine-tuning, and its event/argument
   scoring differs. It supports *testing* trained extraction on held-out families. It does not predict that DREAM's
   binding errors will resist training.
4. **Old benchmark numbers** (OpenPI2.0, MATRES) are historical, task-specific baselines for older models, not a
   current capability ceiling.
5. **Constrained decoding** cannot guarantee source-faithful meaning but can change downstream accuracy and enforce
   some type constraints. "Will not touch DREAM's semantic errors" is too strong.
6. **"Nearly all query research concerns human intent"** is too broad. Several cited tasks involve latent world facts,
   and recent belief-space planners use environmental sensing (below).
7. **Mandatory independent verification** is a system hypothesis, not an established fix. It depends on independent
   evidence, coverage, cost and latency, and on what happens when a truthful reading conflicts with a false encoded
   history. Re-reading the same text is not independent evidence, and DREAM's wrong interpretations already came with
   correct quotations.
8. **"Errors are not artefacts of one prompt or one schema"** is too strong. DREAM measured context and representation
   effects (FULL vs LOCAL; EXCLUSIVE vs FACTORED). The nullable-prior test was **inconclusive** (+0.043 [0, 0.130]).
9. **MERIT:** both its structured fact store and its LLM summarisation beat embedding retrieval on updates, and its
   hybrid was worse than the fact store. That supports careful state-maintenance comparisons, not universal symbolic
   superiority.
10. **REVOIR** is a preprint ("under review"); the NeurIPS acceptance was not verified. **KnowNo** concerns instruction
    disambiguation with fully grounded descriptions. **CASEL** assumes intervention effectiveness, in simulation.
    **VIRF** also tests VLM grounding and injected inconsistencies, so it is broader than "given state only".
11. **DREAM count:** the factored controls contain **19 raw false revisions** (18 in mixed outputs plus 1 accepted),
    not 18.

**Missing recent work (from the audit):**
- **StateGraph / StateChangeBench** (arXiv 2611.02912, 2026-11-02, preprint): dependency-aware state revision, 50
  authored cases with hard negatives and 36 action-consequence labels. Gains in selective cascade revision; no
  action-decision results in this version. The closest neighbour in evaluation design.
- **Belief-State Engine** (arXiv 2609.10036, 2026-09-09, preprint): an external Bayesian belief module for supplied
  POMDPs.
- **Probabilistic visual grounding with symbolic belief-space planning** (arXiv 2609.16884, 2026-09-15, to appear at
  NeuS): uncertain interpretations feed a symbolic planner. Supports keeping alternative world states rather than
  treating one parse as truth; world-state, not intent.
- **Neurosymbolic Embodied Agents** (arXiv 2608.16794v2): exploration acquires predicates before constrained
  planning. The guarantee is conditional on correct grounding.

**What DREAM itself supports (scoped):** a correct engine under supplied finite dynamics; a relative observation-
coverage benefit that survived the text interface (+1.95 on the fixed 24-family mixture); and a representation-and-
compiler gain (0.696→0.852). It does not support learned dynamics, transfer beyond the designed language, general
coreference, noisy or unknown observations, or reliable execution. The 5 unsafe world-goal cases (18 executions)
stay beside the utility result.
